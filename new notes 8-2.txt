Source families — what's missing

On the assessor side, add the Prop 13 base-year value and last-sale date/price if exposed. This matters more in California than almost anything else: assessed value ≠ market value here. A long-tenured owner can have a wildly suppressed assessed value, which means enormous hidden equity that a naive "assessed value trend" won't surface. Also grab tax rate area and improvement square footage if available.

On the recorder side, your foreclosure stack is good, but add lis pendens (litigation pending — active pressure), abstract of judgment (judgment liens), mechanics/tax liens, and affidavit of death (you already flagged has_affidavit_of_death for probate — it belongs in this list as a first-class event, not enrichment). Probate/death is one of the most actionable seller signals you'll find: motivated heirs, often out of area, often wanting a fast cash close.

On tax/auction, add installment-plan status. An owner on a 5-year payment plan under R&T §4217 is trying to keep the property — that's a different (weaker) distress profile than someone ignoring it entirely, and you don't want to score them the same.

Schema — three real gaps

The biggest one: your events table needs grantor and grantee as separate fields, plus documentary transfer tax / consideration. Direction matters — a grant deed from the owner is a sale (exit signal), to the owner is acquisition. And in CA the transfer tax on a deed lets you back out the actual purchase price (roughly $1.10 per $1,000 at the state rate — verify Butte's exact rate, some jurisdictions add to it). Last purchase price + years held + no recent deed of trust = your single strongest equity signal, and it's sitting right there in the recorder data.

Second: you have storage/ bronze writers but no raw table in the schema. Add a raw_fetches (or raw_documents) table with source_url, fetched_at, http_status, content_hash, file_path. That's what actually enforces your "raw capture mandatory, no silent fallbacks" standard — right now it's a directory convention, not a queryable record.

Third: make data gaps first-class. Add a needs_review / data_gaps field on properties and events. Your recurring cover-page credibility problem — rank-1 parcel with no owner name, APN 027-290-021-000 with no assessee — should surface as a scoreable, filterable flag, not a silent blank. That way a missing owner name demotes a parcel automatically instead of embarrassing you later.

Minor: add temporal columns (valid_from/valid_to) on property_owner_links and capture_date on snapshots, and score_version + per-feature contributions on lead_scores so you can tune and debug rather than staring at a single opaque number.

Scoring — one structural change

Your instinct at the bottom ("distress + absentee + equity + recent legal movement, not just one") is the right one, but a flat additive score works against it. A parcel can rack up points on one axis and score "high" while being useless. Make it gated instead of summed: require at least one distress trigger (foreclosure OR tax) AND some equity signal AND some contactability/motivation signal (absentee, probate, entity owner) before a parcel qualifies at all — then score within the qualified set. Distress with zero equity isn't wholesale-able; equity with zero distress isn't motivated.

Two more:

Recency decay on legal events. A cured NOD from 2019 is noise; an NOD from last month is gold. Time-weight the foreclosure stack, or you'll resurface dead leads.
Disqualifiers are as valuable as signals. Recently sold (new owner, not motivated), currently MLS-listed (retail competition), owner-occupied + homestead + current on taxes. Filtering out protects your credibility on the cover page as much as scoring in.
Keep the redemption-removed-late signal (your Gridley catch) — that's precisely the thing the national data vendors miss and it's your proof-of-value hook.
